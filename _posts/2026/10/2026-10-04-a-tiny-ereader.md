I've been using a small eReader, the Xteink X4 Pro, for a couple of weeks and really like it!
<!--more--> 

## A Little Background

I've done the majority of my reading on an eReader since 2010 when I purchased my first eInk device, a Sony PRS-650. Since then I've owned many of the Kobo eReaders starting with a Kobo Touch purchased from Borders as they were going out of business in the summer of 2011. My go to device for the last couple of years has been a Kobo Libra Colour.

Kobo's eReader software is very mature and while they would prefer you consume content from their store, they don't have an adversarial relationship with the community bringing open source alternatives and enhancements to the platform. In the early days, I used some mods but these days the stock software meets all my needs. I do use Calibre for library management and side-loading books on to my devices.

## The X4 Pro

I started seeing articles in my newsfeed, most likely The Verge, Wired and/or Lifehacker, a few weeks ago about pocket-sized eReaders small enough to piggyback on your phone. In fact, they had magnets positioned to do just that! The model most discussed was the X3 but a newer device, the X4 Pro, came out this summer and it had some upgrades, including a touch screen and front light, that were necessary given my history with Kobo devices.

![Size comparison to an iPhone 16 Pro](/assets/2026/10/2026-10-04-a-tiny-ereader/IMG_0813.jpeg){: .align-right}

Needless to say, I was intrigued. The idea of being able to walk around with an eReader in my pocket or attached to the back of my phone was a little exciting. I often throw my Kobo in the car when I know I'll be waiting around at an appointment or while Jill does a little shopping. Taking a few measurements, I knew the X4 Pro would overhang the bottom of my phone a little but thought it would be fine. I ordered one and it arrived about 10 days later.

![reader overhang](/assets/2026/10/2026-10-04-a-tiny-ereader/IMG_0814.jpeg){: .align-left}

It does overhang the bottom of the phone but only a quarter of an inch. It has a 4.3 inch eInk display with a resolution of 800x600 and a pixel density of 219ppi. It weighs about 70 grams (or 2.5 ounces).

## Software

All the articles were critical of the stock software. But that's ok because development is encouraged with this line of devices and an open source project has been releasing software for more than a year targeting the microcontroller used in the devices. That software, Crosspoint Reader, is a great replacement for the software shipped with the device. The late September release (1.6.5) had several updates that really made the reading experience on the device much better (and it was already quite good). In particular, being able to side-load TrueType variable weight fonts and better control over the front light were welcome additions.

Putting books on the device is relatively painless. The same project has developed a Calibre plug-in to use with devices running Crosspoint Reader. Once installed and configured, it's as simple as starting Calibre and choosing the Calibre Wireless file transfer option from the device. Once the device connects, selecting and sending books from within Calibre works the same as side-loading books to other devices.

## Tweaks

I've loaded the Roboto (variable weight version) font on the X4 Pro and my Libra. In the current release (1.6.5), the software offers point sizes from 8 to 22 point in 1 point increments for this variable weight font. In the image below, I have the X4 set to 13pt with the line spacing set to Tight and a screen margin of 5. This results in a very readable (for me) display. On the Libra, I've highlighted the matching text displayed on the X4. At these settings, it looks like I can get about half a page of what I'd normally see on the Libra. So, twice as many page turns but a small price to pay for the portability of the X4 Pro.

![comparing pages between kobo and x4 pro](/assets/2026/10/2026-10-04-a-tiny-ereader/IMG_0454.jpeg)

## Verdict

I've read exclusively on the X4 Pro for the last week or so. I'm on my fourth book with the device. It's comfortable to use for extended sessions because it is so light. The physical buttons work fine for page turns but I find myself swiping on the touch display to move through a book. Will it replace my Kobo? Probably not. The novelty may wear off after a while but it definitely seems like it will be a great eReader to slip in my pocket when I'm on the go!
